## Step 2: Five Essential Applicant Points

### 1. Check EB-2 Eligibility Before Building the Waiver Case

**Summary:** Before seeking the National Interest Waiver, you must qualify for EB-2 through an advanced degree, a bachelor's degree plus five years of progressive experience in your specialty after graduation, or exceptional ability supported by evidence of expertise above the ordinary level. Meeting that baseline does not establish the waiver: you must also show that your work has substantial merit and national importance, that you are well positioned to advance it, and that granting the waiver benefits the United States on balance.

**Common mistake:** Treating an advanced degree as proof that the entire NIW case qualifies.

**Sources:** Local files — [What does “National Interest Waiver” mean?](../source/what-does-niw-actually-mean.md), [Things You Must Know for Your NIW Journey](../source/niw-roadmap-navigating.md); fetched page — [Matter of Dhanasar Explained](https://getniw.com/matter-of-dhanasar-explained/).

### 2. Define the Work You Plan to Do and Why It Matters Beyond Your Employer

**Summary:** Your proposed endeavor is the specific work you intend to pursue in the United States, including the problem you will address and the benefit you expect it to produce. For Prong 1, explain both its value and its broader importance beyond one employer; the importance of your profession alone does not establish the importance of your particular plan.

**Common mistake:** Using a job title or daily duties as the whole description of your proposed endeavor.

**Sources:** Local file — [Decoding the EB-2 NIW](../source/decoding-the-eb-2-niw.md); fetched page — [Matter of Dhanasar Explained](https://getniw.com/matter-of-dhanasar-explained/).

### 3. Connect Your Past Contributions to a Credible Future Plan

**Summary:** For Prong 2, use your personal statement to explain specific future activities and realistic outcomes, connecting them to the experience and skills that prepare you to carry them out. Support that plan with evidence of what you personally contributed to past projects and, where available, genuine collaboration invitations or client interest that explains why others want to work with you.

**Common mistake:** Submitting a career history and team success stories without showing your individual role or how you will put that experience to use.

**Sources:** Fetched pages — [Personal Statement as Prong 2 Evidence](https://getniw.com/personal-statement-prong-2-evidence-uscis-niw/), [Research Collaboration Invitations & Letters of Interest](https://getniw.com/research-collaboration-letters-interest-prong-2-niw/), [Documenting Your Individual Contributions in Team-Based Projects](https://getniw.com/individual-contributions-team-projects-eb2-niw/); local file — [Decoding the EB-2 NIW](../source/decoding-the-eb-2-niw.md).

### 4. Explain Why Granting the Waiver Benefits the United States

**Summary:** The NIW allows self-petitioning without an employer sponsor, but Prong 3 still asks why the benefit of your contributions justifies waiving the usual job-offer and labor-certification requirements, even if qualified U.S. workers are available. If you plan to work independently, explain why an employer-led labor-market test does not fit your work model and support that explanation with evidence of the national benefit of your planned contributions.

**Common mistake:** Relying on a shortage of workers as the entire reason the waiver should be granted.

**Sources:** Fetched pages — [“Even If Other U.S. Workers Are Available”](https://getniw.com/us-workers-available-prong-3-argument-niw/), [The Self-Employment Argument](https://getniw.com/self-employment-labor-certification-impractical-niw/); local file — [What does “National Interest Waiver” mean?](../source/what-does-niw-actually-mean.md).

### 5. Separate Petition Approval from Residency and Work Permission

**Summary:** Filing or receiving approval of Form I-140, the NIW petition, does not itself grant immigration status or permission to work in the United States. The green-card process has a separate residency stage tied to visa availability and your priority date, or place in the queue, with adjustment of status for applicants inside the United States or consular processing for those abroad.

**Common mistake:** Assuming an approved petition lets you remain or work in the United States while waiting for a green card.

**Sources:** Local files — [Things You Must Know for Your NIW Journey](../source/niw-roadmap-navigating.md), [Top 10 Myths About the EB-2 NIW Process Debunked](../source/common-myths-about-eb2-niw-green-card.md), [What does “National Interest Waiver” mean?](../source/what-does-niw-actually-mean.md); fetched pages — none for this point.

## Step 3: Why These Five

I chose these five because they cover the decisions a newcomer must understand first: the eligibility baseline, each of the three waiver requirements, and what the petition does and does not provide. Combining the personal-statement, collaboration, and team-contribution lessons under Prong 2 gives applicants a practical evidence direction without crowding out the process warning. These points take priority over narrower filing and presentation details because they shape the case before document assembly begins. I considered a separate point on recommendation-letter counts and left it out because the local introduction and roadmap suggest different ranges, while their shared emphasis on specific, credible evidence fits within the points above.
